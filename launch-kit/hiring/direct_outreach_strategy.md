# Direct Outreach Strategy For arXiv Help

## Bottom Line

Yes: work backwards from the arXiv submission/endorsement problem.

But do not frame the search geographically. Frame it by:

- recent arXiv activity,
- category/domain fit,
- LLM evaluation or AI safety relevance,
- willingness to review an independent working paper,
- ability to advise on `cs.CL` or `cs.AI` submission.

India, Sri Lanka, and other international research communities may be excellent places to look because there are many active AI/NLP researchers there, often with strong arXiv histories. But the target is not "someone from India." The target is:

> an active AI/NLP/LLM-safety researcher with recent arXiv papers in the right endorsement domain who can legitimately advise, review, and possibly endorse if appropriate.

## Recommended Two-Lane Strategy

### Lane 1: Known / Warm Contacts

Start with people Alex has already interviewed or interacted with.

Best first lead:

- Professor Animesh Mukherjee, IIT Kharagpur.

Why:

- He is a computer science professor.
- His research interests include content governance, moderation, fairness, and responsible AI.
- He has AI ethics and LLM safety-adjacent work.
- He has a prior conversational relationship with Alex.
- He may know students/postdocs who are better practical fits for hands-on arXiv packaging.

Ask him for:

1. a quick read on whether the paper fits `cs.CL` or `cs.AI`,
2. suggestions for improving the framing,
3. a referral to a junior collaborator, postdoc, or PhD student who can help package the paper,
4. endorsement only if he is eligible and comfortable.

Do not lead by asking for endorsement.

### Lane 2: Targeted arXiv/Freelance Search

Use arXiv to find recent papers in:

- LLM sycophancy,
- LLM evaluation,
- LLM-as-judge,
- calibration,
- prompt sensitivity,
- AI safety benchmarks,
- responsible AI / content moderation.

Then identify authors who:

- have recent arXiv submissions in `cs.CL` or `cs.AI`,
- list a public email,
- are postdocs, PhD students, research engineers, or assistant professors,
- may be open to paid consulting/editing work.

Contact them directly with a narrow ask:

> I have a working paper and reproducibility package. I am looking for a paid technical editor/arXiv packaging consultant with LLM evaluation experience. I am not asking for ghostwriting or honorary authorship.

## Why Direct Outreach May Beat Upwork

Upwork is good for LaTeX and editing. It is weaker for endorsement/category strategy.

Direct outreach is better for:

- legitimate arXiv category judgment,
- referrals to eligible endorsers,
- stronger AI/NLP literature fit,
- credibility around LLM evaluation.

Best plan:

Use both.

- Direct outreach to 5-10 relevant researchers.
- Upwork/freelance posting for packaging help.
- Hire the best concrete operator, not necessarily the most credentialed person.

## What To Avoid

Avoid:

- "Can I pay you to endorse me?"
- "Can you be a co-author so arXiv accepts it?"
- "Can you submit this under your account?"
- mass emailing dozens of researchers.

Better:

> I am looking for a paid technical review and arXiv packaging consultation. If, after reviewing the paper, you think it is appropriate for your category and you are eligible to endorse, I would appreciate guidance on the legitimate endorsement process.

## Practical Target Profile

Ideal paid consultant:

- recent arXiv author in `cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, or adjacent AI safety/NLP area,
- can edit LaTeX/BibTeX,
- understands LLM evaluation,
- willing to provide a short paid readiness memo first,
- may be able to advise on endorsement,
- does not ask for authorship unless contributing real scholarship.

## First Milestone Offer

Offer a small paid first step:

> $150-$400 for a 2-3 page arXiv-readiness memo covering category fit, moderation risk, references, reproducibility gaps, and whether the paper is close to submit-ready.

Then decide whether to hire for full package work.
