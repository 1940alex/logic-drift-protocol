# What To Buy And What Not To Buy

## Buy This

Pay for:

- technical editing,
- LaTeX/BibTeX conversion,
- citation verification,
- figure/caption polish,
- arXiv category strategy,
- moderation-risk review,
- reproducibility-package cleanup,
- help identifying legitimate endorsers,
- final submission checklist.

This is normal and useful. It moves the paper off the critical path.

## Do Not Buy This

Do not pay for:

- fake co-authorship,
- an endorsement from someone who has not read or understood the paper,
- guaranteed arXiv acceptance,
- mass-email endorsement campaigns,
- someone submitting under their own name as a proxy,
- fabricated institutional affiliation,
- citations or claims added only for prestige.

That is the wrong kind of shortcut. It can damage credibility and create avoidable arXiv/account risk.

## Co-Author Rule

Someone should become a co-author only if they make a real scholarly contribution, such as:

- redesigning the method,
- materially improving the analysis,
- adding new experiments,
- contributing a substantial literature synthesis,
- taking responsibility for part of the argument.

Formatting, editing, citation cleanup, or arXiv packaging should normally be acknowledged, not rewarded with co-authorship.

## Endorsement Rule

An arXiv endorser is not a paid reviewer and not a prestige prop. arXiv says endorsers should be knowledgeable in the subject area and should only endorse when they are confident the submitter will submit high-quality research in the relevant area.

The clean strategy is:

1. Make the paper strong enough to be endorsable.
2. Identify relevant recent arXiv authors in `cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, or adjacent areas.
3. Ask a small number respectfully with the paper and artifact package attached.
4. Use a contractor to help identify candidates and prepare the request, not to fake the endorsement.
